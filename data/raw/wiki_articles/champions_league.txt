UEFA Champions League: Europe's Premier Club Competition

The UEFA Champions League, commonly referred to simply as the Champions League, is the premier annual club football competition organized by the Union of European Football Associations (UEFA). It is contested by the top-division football clubs from UEFA's member associations and is the most prestigious and financially lucrative club competition in the world.

The competition began in 1955 as the European Champion Clubs' Cup, often called the European Cup. The format was reorganized and rebranded as the UEFA Champions League in 1992, adopting a group stage format that allowed more clubs to participate and increased revenue through broadcasting rights.

Real Madrid has been the most successful club in the history of the competition, winning it a record 15 times. Their five consecutive wins from 1956 to 1960, led by the legendary Alfredo Di Stéfano and Ferenc Puskás, established them as the dominant force of European football. In the modern era, Real Madrid won four titles in five seasons between 2014 and 2018 under managers Carlo Ancelotti and Zinedine Zidane.

AC Milan is the second-most successful club with seven titles. Their wins in 1989 and 1990, under manager Arrigo Sacchi with stars like Ruud Gullit, Marco van Basten, and Frank Rijkaard, are considered among the finest achievements in European football. Milan's 2003 and 2007 wins came under Carlo Ancelotti.

Liverpool has won the trophy six times, including memorable victories in 1977, 1978, 1981, and 1984 under legendary manager Bob Paisley. Their most recent triumph came in 2019 in Madrid, when they defeated Tottenham Hotspur 2-0, with goals from Mohamed Salah and Divock Origi. Jürgen Klopp managed that Liverpool side, which featured Virgil van Dijk, who had transformed the club's defense since his £75 million transfer from Southampton.

The 1999 final is remembered as one of the most dramatic in history. Manchester United, managed by Sir Alex Ferguson, were losing 1-0 to Bayern Munich in injury time when Teddy Sheringham and Ole Gunnar Solskjaer scored two goals in the 90th and 93rd minutes to complete a stunning comeback. United became the last English club to win the treble.

Cristiano Ronaldo holds the record as the all-time top scorer in the competition with over 140 goals, having scored for Manchester United, Real Madrid, and Juventus. The competition's current format, which expanded to 36 clubs in 2024, allows for a league phase before the knockout rounds.
