Zinedine Zidane: Elegance Personified on the Pitch and Touchline

Zinedine Yazid Zidane, born on June 23, 1972, in Marseille, France, to Algerian immigrant parents, is widely regarded as one of the greatest footballers ever to have played the game. His elegant style, technical mastery, and ability to perform in the biggest moments made him a player of incomparable quality.

Zidane began his professional career at Cannes before moving to Bordeaux, where his performances attracted attention from the continent's biggest clubs. In 1996, Juventus signed him, and in three seasons in Turin, he won two Serie A titles alongside Alessandro Del Piero and Didier Deschamps. His performances earned him the FIFA World Player of the Year award in 1998.

Zidane's crowning achievement as a player came at the 1998 FIFA World Cup on home soil in France. He scored twice with headers in the final against Brazil, helping France win their first World Cup. That French side, managed by Aimé Jacquet, also included Thierry Henry, Patrick Vieira, and Marcel Desailly. Two years later, Zidane helped France win Euro 2000 in a tournament where he was named the best player.

In 2001, Real Madrid paid Juventus £47.2 million for Zidane — the highest transfer fee ever at the time. At the Bernabéu, he delivered perhaps the greatest goal ever scored in a Champions League final. His volley against Bayer Leverkusen in the 2002 final in Glasgow was struck with his weaker left foot and described by many as the perfect football goal. Real Madrid won 2-1, giving Zidane a Champions League winner's medal to add to his World Cup and European Championship.

The 2006 World Cup in Germany was Zidane's final tournament. He came out of international retirement to lead France to the final, winning the Golden Ball as the tournament's best player. However, the final against Italy ended in tragedy — Zidane headbutted Marco Materazzi following provocation and was sent off in extra time. France lost on penalties. It was a bittersweet farewell to one of football's most gifted players.

After retiring, Zidane joined Real Madrid's coaching staff and in January 2016 was given the first team. He immediately proved his tactical acumen, guiding Real Madrid to three consecutive Champions League titles in 2016, 2017, and 2018 — a feat never before achieved in the competition's history. Luka Modric, Toni Kroos, and Casemiro formed one of the most dominant midfield trios ever assembled under Zidane's management.
