Bayern Munich: Germany's Eternal Champions

FC Bayern München, founded in 1900 in Munich, Germany, is the most successful football club in German football history and one of the most decorated clubs in Europe. The club, known as the Rekordmeister (record champions), plays at the Allianz Arena and has dominated the Bundesliga since the competition's inception in 1963.

Bayern Munich's European pedigree is extensive. Under coach Udo Lattek and then Dettmar Cramer in the 1970s, Bayern won three consecutive European Cups from 1974 to 1976, with Franz Beckenbauer captaining the side. Beckenbauer, the legendary libero, played the role of a ball-playing defender decades before the concept became mainstream. The side also featured Gerd Müller, arguably the most lethal penalty-box striker in football history, who scored 68 goals in 62 international appearances for West Germany.

Beckenbauer later won the FIFA World Cup with West Germany as a manager in 1990, becoming one of only three people — along with Didier Deschamps and Mario Zagallo — to win the World Cup as both player and manager.

The 2012-13 season under Jupp Heynckes produced one of the greatest domestic and European campaigns in football history. Bayern won the Bundesliga by a record 25 points, reached the Champions League final, and won the DFB Pokal, completing a treble. In the Champions League final on home soil at Wembley, they defeated Borussia Dortmund 2-1, with Arjen Robben scoring the winning goal with his left foot in extra time.

Pep Guardiola managed Bayern from 2013 to 2016, winning the Bundesliga in each of his three seasons and introducing a more possession-oriented style. However, despite reaching the Champions League semi-finals three times, Guardiola could not deliver European glory to Munich. His successor Carlo Ancelotti won the Bundesliga title before being sacked.

Robert Lewandowski's eight seasons at Bayern from 2014 to 2022 were marked by extraordinary consistency. He became the club's all-time top scorer and in the 2020-21 season broke Gerd Müller's 49-year-old Bundesliga record by scoring 41 goals in a single season. The pandemic-affected 2019-20 season saw Bayern win the Champions League under Hans-Dieter Flick, with Lewandowski, Thomas Müller, and Serge Gnabry starring in a 8-2 quarter-final demolition of Barcelona.

Harry Kane joined from Tottenham Hotspur in 2023, winning the Bundesliga in his debut season and becoming one of the Bundesliga's most prolific scorers. Vincent Kompany was appointed manager in 2024 following Thomas Tuchel's departure.
