FIFA World Cup: The Greatest Show on Earth

The FIFA World Cup is the international football championship contested by the national football teams of the member associations of FIFA. Held every four years, it is the most widely viewed and followed sporting event in the world, watched by billions of people across the globe.

The inaugural World Cup was held in Uruguay in 1930, with the host nation defeating Argentina 4-2 in the final. Brazil is the most successful nation in World Cup history, having won the tournament five times: in 1958, 1962, 1970, 1994, and 2002. The 1958 tournament in Sweden introduced the world to a 17-year-old phenomenon named Pelé, who became the youngest goalscorer in World Cup final history.

Brazil's 1970 side, featuring Pelé, Jairzinho, Tostão, and Rivelino, is widely considered the greatest national team ever assembled. Captain Carlos Alberto scored arguably the greatest goal in World Cup history in the final against Italy, finishing off a sweeping team move.

Germany and Italy are joint second with four titles each. Germany's 2014 triumph in Brazil, sealed by Mario Götze's extra-time winner against Argentina, was particularly notable for their 7-1 semifinal demolition of hosts Brazil — a result that shocked the world and is still referred to as the "Mineirazo" in Brazil.

Argentina's triumphs came in 1978, hosted domestically under controversial circumstances, and 1986 in Mexico, where Diego Maradona delivered arguably the greatest individual World Cup performance. Maradona scored five goals and provided five assists, including the infamous "Hand of God" goal and the "Goal of the Century" — a solo run from his own half — against England in the quarter-finals.

The 2022 World Cup in Qatar saw Lionel Messi finally claim the one trophy that had eluded him. Argentina defeated France 4-2 on penalties after a thrilling 3-3 draw in regular and extra time. Messi scored twice in the final, including a penalty, and Mbappé scored a hat-trick for France. Messi won the Golden Ball as tournament MVP for the second time, having also won it in 2014.

Ronaldo and Messi, the two dominant players of their generation, both participated in multiple World Cups. Ronaldo won the Golden Boot at the 2006 tournament where Portugal finished third. The World Cup, unlike club football, gave these two rivals equal standing — neither has won it more than once.
